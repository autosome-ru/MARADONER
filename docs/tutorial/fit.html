Parameters estimation

Fitting model to data

The parameters of the model are estimated with the fit command. Briefly, if we denote the expression table as $Y$ and the loading matrix as $B$, MARADONER models the expression as $$Y = μ_p 1_s^T + 1_p μ_s^T + B U + E.$$ Here, $μ_p$ and $μ_s$ are promoter-wise and sample-wise means, $U$ is a matrix of motif activities and $E$ is noise. The activities are random. For motif $k$ in a sample of group $g$, $$U_{k} \,~\, N(μ_{m,k},\: τ_k ν_g),\: E \,~\, N(0,\: σ_g).$$ So each motif has a mean activity $μ_m$ and a variance $τ$, and each group of samples has an error variance $σ$ and a multiplier of activity variances $ν$. These are the parameters to be estimated. The activities are dealt with later by the predict command.

The command requires only the project name:

> maradoner fit MyProject

After a successful fit run, a *.fit file should appear in the current working directory. Files of the later steps left from a previous fit of the same project are removed.

The parameters are estimated one kind after another, and each stage reports itself as it starts:

Stage Parameters Method
Error variances $σ$ of each group Restricted maximum likelihood (REML) on the part of the data orthogonal to the loadings
Promoter-wise means $μ_p$ Maximum likelihood, the solution of the minimal norm
Variances of motif activities $τ$ of each motif, $ν$ of each group REML on the data with all means projected out
Motif means $μ_m$ Generalized least squares
Sample means $μ_s$ Generalized least squares

The reasoning behind this order is explained in the How it works section. The output of a run is discussed in the Quickstart.

Warnings

The fit command checks whether the data are enough to estimate what was asked for. The warnings below are the most common ones.

Warning Meaning Remedy
The error variances of the sample groups are not identifiable There are more groups than the samples can support. That happens when every sample forms a separate group and samples are few Fewer and larger groups (--sample-groups of create), or more samples
Variances of motif activities were estimated as zero The data carry little signal about most motifs. With the default settings, predict leaves such motifs out Fewer motifs, clustering or --no-motif-variance
The Fisher information for (tau, nu) is singular Some variances can't be told apart. A common cause is collinear motifs, i.e. a loading matrix of a rank lower than the number of motifs. Standard errors of the variances and the tests built on them are unreliable Removing duplicated motifs, or clustering
The variance of motif activities of a sample group was estimated as zero The activities of the group are shrunk to the motif means, and its tests are uninformative. The group may be too small or too noisy Merging the group with a similar one

Variance structure

By default, each motif has a variance and each group has an error variance and a multiplier. The options below change that.

Name Description Default value
--no-motif-variance One variance for all motifs. This makes the model closer to the classic (IS)MARA and might be beneficial when the data is small --motif-variance
--error-lowrank <int> Rank $k$ of a low-rank correction to the covariance of the noise across samples: it becomes $D + W W^T$ with $W$ of $s$ rows and $k$ columns. Captures correlated structure of samples missed by the diagonal $D$, e.g. a batch or an unmodeled covariate. Must be smaller than the number of samples minus 1 0
--activity-lowrank <int> Rank $k$ of a low-rank correction to the covariance of the activities across samples: it becomes $G + V V^T$, with $G$ being a diagonal matrix of $ν$. Captures hidden factors of samples along which motif activities change together, as explained in the Hidden covariates section. Requires motif variances 0
--promoter-variance (Experimental) Promoters are reweighted by a heuristic estimate of their individual error variances Disabled
The paper describes a REML estimator of promoter variances. The --promoter-variance option does a different thing at the moment, namely a mixture-based reweighting of promoters. The weights define no likelihood, so the likelihood-ratio test of the ANOVA is replaced with a Wald test for such fits.

The low-rank corrections enter the estimation of variances only. The motif means and their standard errors are still computed under the model with diagonal covariances, and MARADONER prints a note about it.

Clustering

Motifs with similar loadings are hard to tell apart, and with more motifs than promoters the model can't be estimated at all. In those cases the motifs can be replaced with clusters:

> maradoner fit MyProject --clustering KMeans --num-clusters 100

Here, KMeans puts each motif into exactly one cluster. The alternative is NMF, a non-negative matrix factorization: a motif may then belong to several clusters, and the loadings must be non-negative. The number of clusters is 200 by default.

All the later commands then work with clusters in place of motifs, and the clusters are named cluster_1, cluster_2 and so on. At the moment, the composition of clusters is not among the exported tables.

Held-out promoters

A part of promoters can be kept out of the fit as a testing set, either by chromosome or by name:

> maradoner fit MyProject --test-chromosomes 3 --test-chromosomes X
> maradoner fit MyProject --test-promoters-filename names.txt

The first form needs promoter names that contain a chromosome, e.g. chr3:1000-2000. The second one takes a text file with one promoter name per line. What to do with the testing set next is explained in the Goodness of fit section.
